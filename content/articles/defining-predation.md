This is a work-in-progress writeout of how relationships should be viewed, and what is predatory behavior, as well as my extension of the term pedophilia.

It's certainly difficult to talk about relationships and abuse without upsetting some people. Some are rightfully upset, a lot of them are not. I'd like to set my record on how relationships should be defined, and when to reconsider a bond with somebody.

## Malice Vs. Flaw

Of course this one is a doozy. As a subscriber to Deontology well before I found out about it, the core principle is the intent. If somebody has malicious intent, it's set and stone: The relationship is not legitimate, and one should likely run away, unless they have a cynicism about their situation or are okay with pretending along, which if you have to think about it, it's very likely not your case.

That being said, a flawed individual is still damaging nonetheless. One must always consider whether it's in their best interest to continue a relationship with somebody that has certain character flaws. It's understandable to think of this as being selfish, but self-preservation is a thing I am perfecly okay with someone choosing. I've been there, feeling like I owed people my attention and heart, damaging me, but giving me a boost of happiness in seeing somebody else get happier due to my company. It's that balance that I'm talking about, neither of the two extremes work out in the end. Being overly selfish creates a monster, being overly selfless creates a husk, both undateable. 

Some flaws are less heavy than others, of course. Somebody whose flaws are violent or manipulative is much more dangerous than somebody who is overly worried, clingy or who does not understand certain cues, or is bad at separating their love life and other lives. Some are fixable, some are not, and that difference is not entirely relevant. Even fixable flaws don't necessitate you stay with someone.

Notice one thing: For people to consider these aspects, they must have the potential to think about them. Which is why I'd like to make an adendum on how I define the term "pedophilia".

## Defining Pedophilia

It's certainly a simple task, is it not? Well, not really. I won't even get into the whole "oh there's three words for certain parts of puberty", the main concept is: Pedophilia is a psychiatric disorder in which somebody experiences primary or exclusive sexual attraction to (prepubescent) children. Coloquially, we extend that. Somebody suffering from a disorder is not inherently evil, those people are the ones who act on their illness or are actually evil, seeking to corrupt an innocent mind and cause damage for sadistic sexual purposes. Nonetheless, we put these two people, the sufferers and the actors, under the same term. I think it would be productive to simply understand this nuance, as demonizing those with the illness only makes them unable to seek help or, worse, they might think the only way forward is to align themselves with the actor group, potentially acting on their urges. 

No, I am not saying normalize pedophilia, what I am saying is maintain the disorder's pathological aspect, but add complexity to its (rightful) social stigma. Those with pedophilic thoughts should be helped instead of alienated and thrown into 4chan. Should we have different words? I don't know. It would make sense to say somebody "suffers from pedophilia" as its morally neutral version, while a "pedophile" would be the morally horrible version. We already sort of do this when we say "child predator", and I don't think some major semantic reform needs to happen, just pay closer attention and consider whether somebody is past the point of salvation or not.

To a small degree, I'm speaking from experience. Before I understood the full extent of OCD and broke down this specific mechanism, I had spurts of self-hatred and paranoia due to intrusive thoughts of generally sexually deviant nature. My mind has disturbed me with horrible unwanted intrusive thoughts resembling online harassment I was already very familiar with due to being visibly queer on the internet, especially Discord. Thoughts such as "what if those people are right? What if I'm just some evil pedophile creep and I need to leave this world?" 

It did not need to be true, only damaging. I never struggled with any inner urges or anything of the sort, and my brain knew that, which is why it picked such a horrible thing to intrude with. I'm not sure what percentage of what I'm saying here is OCD versus Autism versus ADHD (what a horrible, misleading name!), but part of my intrusive thought schedule is exactly that: Taking something morally nuanced, like your attraction growing up alongside you, that you are absolutely certain about, and exacerbating it to a simple, horrible doubt that comes with its own conclusion. I liked teenagers as a teenager, and now at twenty-two I would not have a relationship with anyone under twenty. Between those, there's a veil of vaguenesses; Some benign, some harmful. (What is this talk about a "vagueness"? Well, it'll be here: [On Morality | Vaguenesses](../on-morality#Vaguenesses))

When I was seventeen, I did not have an issue with my equal-aged classmates having relationships with a fifteen or sixteen year old, even if I did not have an interest in anyone younger than me. Then there was, let's call him Jordan, who at seventeen hung around with girls aged thirteen, fourteen. Said horrible, disgusting things about them. That's different. That's fucking evil. And yes, he's still evil to this day and I do not believe he has stopped that interest, not that I can do much living in Brazil as our backwards laws say children are able to consent to sexual acts from fourteen years old onward, don't get me started on my country's takes on this. That evil is much easier to act on if the ages are like Jordan's case, but his evil would have been the same had he behaved the same way to his fellow classmates. So many factors.

I must emphasize, my intrusive thoughts did not originate from actual urges or from sexualizing children, so I don't know exactly what it's like to have the mind of a pedophile. What I'm saying is, I understand the self-hatred associated with the stigma around sexual disorders, and my take is always one resembling the sentence "what I have suffered, I wish nobody would suffer". Perhaps if the topic had a known and dedicated space for conversation and help, I would have figured it all out much sooner and not struggled with such strong paranoid thoughts for years.

### An extension of "pedophilia"

Predation does not necessitate the victim be a child. I see other kinds of people as morally identical to a child predator, such as those who actively seek younger or neurodivergent adults to abuse a power imbalance. Those people should not be called anything else. What we mean in society by pedophilia also includes the overarching mentality of the urge to corrupt an innocent, impressionable mind, bend it to the actor's will. So I say, if a forty-year-old explicitly seeks young adults, or worse, treads the age of consent laws to a tee, they are a pedophile, derogatory. It does NOT matter if they have a psychiatric disorder in which blablablah, they're using the exact same mentality against a victim whose only non-physical difference from a child is their ability to fight back. Growing up makes you more resistant to certain behaviors, but nobody is immune to manipulation, coercion or violence, mental or physical. I've had several adult women tell me horror stories of how the manipulated mind becomes dependent on that very manipulation, and then I experienced it myself. It's a horrible mindset to be in, it nearly dragged me to self harm, it made me cripplingly psychotic, and I felt ill in anxiety when I was not in direct contact with my abuser. I did not need to be a child to be severely traumatized by somebody taking advantage of me.

## Intention and Plausibility

I talk a lot about intent. There's situations, obviously, where the intent cannot be known. In this case, I apply a sort of graph of plausibility. What that means is, what are the chances that somebody is morally sound and does not have evil intentions in a relationship, or any act for that matter? It's not that hard to me: The larger the difference between two people on a factor, the higher the likelihood it will be abused. Take age for example: An eighteen year old has had a lick of adulthood, a taste. Compare that to somebody aged twenty-six. It does not matter much how different their lives were up until that point; A twenty-six year old has had important, life-changing experiences an eighteen-year-old has not, even if only from social perception. Perhaps a twenty-six year old has been in an abusive relationship before, or is more familiar with the outside world's manipulation tactics, any and all factors are taken into account here, but one certainty is that an eighteen year old is easily abused by somebody much older than them. So, with that in mind, it's basic as a linear graph. Eighteen and twenty? A-OK. Twenty-two? Sure... Twenty-four? Uh, not the best... Twenty-six? Likely malicious. Thirty or over? Extremely slim chances of there not being abuse. It is still the intention that matters, but these assumptions are very useful, even if not 100% accurate, because they work separately from what the actor claims to believe, and still may take a record or history into account. 

Another factor here is a presupposed "baseline adulthood". A thirty-five year old is not fundamentally much more of an adult than a twenty-five year old in a lot of cases, so I do not see such a gap as certainly problematic. I don't really think this nuance needs to be explained much further, but essentially, a baseline amount of life experience and maturity may imply an individual can accept a relationship from somebody considerably older without much issue.
